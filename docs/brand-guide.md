# SCAiL Brand Guide

**Version 1.0 — March 2026**

---

## Brand Name

**SCAiL**

Full expansion: South Carolina AI Literacy
Alternative expansion: Southern Corridor AI Literacy Program

### Spelling Rules

The name is always spelled **SCAiL** — capital S, capital C, capital A, lowercase i, capital L.

This is not a typo. The lowercase "i" is intentional and meaningful. It embeds the word "AI" within the name itself: SC**Ai**L. The name visually encodes the mission.

| Context | Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| Logo / wordmark | SCAiL | SCAIL, Scail, scail, Scal |
| Body copy | SCAiL | SCAIL, The SCAIL nonprofit |
| Acronym reference | (SCAiL) | (SCAIL) |
| Domain / URL | scail.org (lowercase exception) | — |

When in doubt: the lowercase "i" is always lowercase, everything else is always uppercase.

## Color Usage Rules

### Do

- Use Navy as the dominant color in headers, navigation bars, and primary call-to-action buttons.
- Use Gold sparingly to draw attention to moments of celebration, achievement, or warmth — award highlights, grant wins, testimonial pull quotes.
- Use Teal for anything related to AI, technology, or innovation — feature callouts, links, icon accents in tech sections, hover states.
- Use Light Gray `#F8FAFC` for alternating section backgrounds to create visual rhythm without introducing new color.
- Use White as the primary content background in light mode.
- Use Near-black `#0F172A` as the background in dark mode, with Slate text `#334155` replaced by lighter grays for legibility.

### Do Not

- Do not use Gold as a background color for large areas — it becomes aggressive at scale.
- Do not use Teal for primary navigation or trust-critical elements — it reads as "tech startup," which undercuts the nonprofit warmth.
- Do not swap phase colors (blue/green/amber) with brand colors in phase-specific UI elements. They serve a data visualization function.
- Do not use more than two brand colors in any single UI component.
- Do not apply gradients between brand colors — the palette is flat by design.

### Contrast and Accessibility

- Navy on White: passes WCAG AA and AAA for all text sizes.
- White on Navy: passes WCAG AA and AAA for all text sizes.
- Gold on Navy: passes WCAG AA for large text; check contrast for small body copy.
- Teal on White: passes WCAG AA for large text; use Slate or Navy for small body copy instead.
- Never place Gold text on White backgrounds for body copy — insufficient contrast.

## Typography

### Typeface

**Inter** is the sole typeface for SCAiL across all digital surfaces.

Inter is open source, widely available, renders cleanly at small sizes, and is already in wide use in the Vite + React ecosystem. It communicates clarity and modernity without cold sterility.

| Role | Weight | Size Range | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / Hero | Bold (700) | 48px–72px | Page titles, hero headlines |
| Section Heading | Semibold (600) | 28px–40px | H2 section headers |
| Subheading | Semibold (600) | 20px–24px | H3 card titles, panel headers |
| Body | Regular (400) | 15px–17px | Paragraphs, descriptions |
| Caption / Label | Medium (500) | 12px–14px | Tags, metadata, timestamps |
| Button | Semibold (600) | 14px–16px | CTA buttons, nav items |

### Monospace

For code samples, technical content, or data displays: use **JetBrains Mono** or fall back to the system monospace stack (`ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, Monaco, Consolas`).

### Line Height

- Headings: 1.2
- Body copy: 1.6
- UI labels and buttons: 1.0–1.2

### Letter Spacing

- Display headings: -0.02em (tight)
- Body: 0 (default)
- All-caps labels: 0.05em–0.1em

## Voice and Tone

### Core Principles

**Warm.** We are talking to real people in real communities. Every word should feel like it comes from a neighbor who genuinely wants to help, not a grant writer trying to impress a board.

**Direct.** Say the thing. Short sentences. Active verbs. If it takes more than two readings to understand, rewrite it.

**Empowering.** The communities we serve are not broken. They are overlooked. The framing is always abundance and capability, never deficit and charity.

**Honest.** We don't overpromise. We say what we're doing and why. If something didn't work, we say that too.

### We / The Organization

Always use "we" when referring to SCAiL in first person. Never write "the organization" or "the nonprofit" in external-facing content — it creates distance.

- Correct: "We believe every community deserves access to AI education."
- Incorrect: "The organization believes every community deserves access to AI education."

### Action Words

Prefer active, concrete verbs. SCAiL is a doing organization.

- Build, teach, grow, launch, connect, fund, equip, support, partner, learn, train, invest, create, run.
- Avoid: facilitate, leverage, operationalize, synergize, deploy (in non-technical contexts), utilize.

### Plain Language First

Every technical term gets a plain-language explanation on first use. AI concepts should be introduced as tools that serve people, not as abstract forces or threats.

- Not: "We leverage large language models to democratize access to AI-adjacent skillsets."
- Yes: "We teach people how AI tools work — and how to use them to get ahead."

### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Grant applications | Professional, precise, evidence-based — but still human |
| Website copy | Warm, energetic, clear |
| Workshop materials | Conversational, encouraging, patient |
| Press / media | Clear, credible, mission-forward |
| Social media | Direct, optimistic, community-first |
| Legal / compliance | Formal, accurate, complete |

### What We Never Sound Like

- Condescending ("These communities may not have heard of AI yet...")
- Savior-complex ("We're bringing AI to the overlooked...")
- Overly academic ("The epistemological implications of...")
- Corporate ("Our go-to-market strategy for Phase 2 deliverables...")
- Vague ("We work to create positive impact in communities...")

## Logo Usage Notes

### The Lowercase "i"

The lowercase "i" in SCAiL is the logo's primary design element. It carries the entire conceptual weight of the brand name — it is where "AI" lives inside the word.

In any logo treatment, the "i" should be visually distinguished in some way: a different color (Teal `#2EC4B6` is recommended), a subtle icon treatment (neural node, circuit dot, connected line), or a deliberate weight shift. The distinction should feel embedded, not bolted on.

### Clear Space

Maintain clear space on all four sides of the logo equal to the height of the lowercase "i" in the wordmark. Nothing — text, images, other logos, page edges — should intrude into this space.

### Minimum Size

- Digital: no smaller than 120px wide for the full wordmark, 32px for the icon-only mark.
- Print: no smaller than 1 inch wide for the full wordmark.

### Approved Backgrounds

| Background | Treatment |
|---|---|
| White `#FFFFFF` | Navy wordmark, Gold and Teal accents |
| Light Gray `#F8FAFC` | Navy wordmark, Gold and Teal accents |
| Navy `#1B365D` | White or Gold wordmark, Teal accent |
| Near-black `#0F172A` | White wordmark, Gold and Teal accents |

### What to Avoid

- Do not place the logo on busy photographic backgrounds without a solid color backing.
- Do not recolor the logo outside the approved palette.
- Do not stretch, compress, rotate, or add drop shadows to the logo.
- Do not use the logo at sizes where the "i" treatment becomes illegible — fall back to the wordmark-only version.
- Do not add the tagline to the logo in contexts where it is already present in surrounding copy.

### Tagline

**"AI Literacy for Every Community"**

The tagline is optional and should only appear in formal contexts (full logo lockup, print materials, grant cover pages). It is set in Inter Regular, in Slate `#334155` or White depending on background, at approximately 40% of the wordmark's height.
