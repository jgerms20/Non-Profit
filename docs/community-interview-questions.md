# Community Interview Questions

**A conversation guide, not a script.**
*For Joshua German + future SCAiL team. Based on Kinsey Meggett's advice to be in the communities before building for them.*

---

## How to Use This Document

These are **open questions designed to make people talk**, not survey questions with checkboxes. The goal is to hear how people actually talk about AI in their own words, on their own terms.

### Ground rules for yourself:

1. **You are listening, not teaching.** If someone says something factually wrong about AI, resist the urge to correct them in the moment. Write it down. Their misconception is data.
2. **Silence is fine.** Ask a question, then wait. People often say the most important thing after a pause.
3. **Follow their thread.** If they go off-topic, go with them for a bit. The "off-topic" is often the real topic.
4. **Never promise anything you can't deliver.** Don't say "We'll bring a workshop to your church!" until you know you can.
5. **Always ask for permission before recording.** If recording, keep it to audio only, and be ready to turn it off at any moment.
6. **Keep it to 30–45 minutes.** Any longer and you're taking advantage of their time.
7. **Thank them with something concrete.** A coffee, a handwritten note, a promise to share what you learned.

### Practical setup:

- Meet them where they are. Their living room, their shop, their church, their kitchen table, the library. Not Starbucks. Not a "coffee chat." Their turf.
- Bring a notebook and a pen. Write things down visibly — it signals respect.
- Don't bring a laptop unless you're demo-ing something at the end and they've asked for it.
- If possible, record audio (with permission) so you're not staring at your notebook the whole time.

## Part 1: Warm-Up (5 minutes)

These questions are not about AI. They're about the person. Do not skip them.

1. Tell me a little about yourself — where are you from, and what keeps you busy most days?
2. How long have you lived in [their town/community]? What do you love about it? What's changed lately?
3. What's been on your mind this week — big or small, doesn't have to be serious?

*Listen for: family, work, community ties, frustrations, recent events. This is where you learn their language.*

---

## Part 2: Technology in Their Daily Life (5–10 minutes)

Broad questions about how they already use (or don't use) technology. Still not asking about AI directly.

4. What do you use your phone for most? Walk me through a normal day.
5. Is there anything about your phone or computer that frustrates you? Or that you wish worked differently?
6. Who in your life do you call when something tech-related isn't working — and why that person?
7. Has anyone in your family — kids, grandkids, neighbors — tried to teach you something about a new app or gadget lately? How'd that go?

*Listen for: their existing relationship with technology, who they trust to learn from, what already frustrates them. These are the hooks SCAiL curriculum should grab.*

---

## Part 3: AI Specifically (10–15 minutes)

Now you get to the point. Notice the phrasing — you're asking about their experience, not their opinion. Experience is more honest.

8. When you hear the phrase "artificial intelligence" or "AI," what's the first thing that comes to mind?
9. Have you ever used something like ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Alexa, or Siri? What was that like?
10. Where do you hear about AI most — the news, social media, friends, church, work?
11. Has AI ever come up in a conversation with someone you know? What were they saying about it?
12. Has someone you know ever been scammed, or almost scammed, by something that might have involved AI? (Phishing, fake voices, fake videos, fake emails from family members.)
13. When you think about AI and your kids or grandkids, what goes through your mind?
14. When you think about AI and your job (or the jobs in your town), what goes through your mind?

*Listen for: fear vs. curiosity ratio, specific scam stories, whether they distinguish AI from "computers" or "tech" at all, how they hear about it. Most people's AI mental model is based on 1–3 stories they've been told. Find out what those stories are.*

---

## Part 4: What They Actually Want (5–10 minutes)

This is where the curriculum design insight comes from. Ask carefully.

15. If someone was going to teach you (or your community) about AI, what would you actually want to learn first?
16. What would make you want to show up to a free workshop about AI? What would make you NOT show up?
17. Who in your life would benefit most from knowing more about this — and why?
18. Where in your community do people already gather when there's something to learn? (Church, library, rec center, barber shop, community meeting, school.)
19. If a friend told you "I'm going to a free AI class next week," how would you react? Would you ask to come, or would you be worried about them?
20. What's one thing AI could help you with tomorrow if you knew how to use it?

*Listen for: venue preferences, trusted voices, the specific problems they want solved. Their answer to #20 is gold — it's the curriculum hook you should lead with.*

---

## Part 5: Closing + Network (5 minutes)

21. Is there anything about this conversation you wish I had asked but didn't?
22. Is there anyone in your life you think I should also talk to about this? Would you be comfortable introducing me?
23. How would you like to hear back from me about what I learn from these conversations?

*The goal of this section: leave the door open, get a warm intro, and make them feel heard.*

## Variant Questions by Audience

Use the full 23 questions above as the base. Add or substitute from the following when you know who you're talking to.

### For Seniors (65+)

- Has anyone ever called you pretending to be a grandchild or family member? What happened?
- What do you watch on TV or read online about "scams"? Have they mentioned AI?
- What would make you feel safer online if you could wave a magic wand?
- Do you worry about AI replacing people at the businesses you rely on (banks, doctors, grocery stores)?

### For Small Business Owners

- What's the one task in your business you wish you could hand off tomorrow?
- Have you tried using AI for anything in your business? What worked, what didn't?
- What would you pay for, and what do you think should be free, when it comes to business tools?
- Who are you competing against that you think is using AI more effectively than you?

### For Parents of K–12 Kids

- What do you know about whether your child is using AI for homework?
- Has the school communicated any kind of AI policy? Do you understand it?
- What's your biggest fear about AI in education? What's your biggest hope?
- If there was a free family AI class at the library on a Saturday morning, would you bring your kids?

### For Farmers / Agricultural Workers

- What are the biggest frustrations in your operation right now — weather, labor, costs, regulations, pests?
- Have you heard about "precision agriculture" or AI farming tools? Where?
- If someone handed you a free tool that could reduce your fertilizer costs by 10%, what would you want to see before you trusted it?
- Where do you go for information — county extension, trade magazines, neighbors, YouTube?

### For Church Leaders / Faith Community Leaders

- How has technology changed your congregation in the last 5 years?
- Do you talk about AI or technology from the pulpit or in small groups? What do you say?
- What ethical or moral questions do you think AI raises for your community?
- If you hosted a free AI safety workshop in the fellowship hall, who in your congregation would come?

### For Young People (14–22)

- How are you using AI right now — be honest?
- What do your friends say about AI that your parents don't know?
- What's one thing you wish adults understood about how you use AI?
- If you could build one thing with AI for your school or community, what would it be?

## Reddit & Online Sentiment: A Supplementary Gauge

Direct community conversations are the gold standard. But for a broader sentiment read, also periodically scan:

### South Carolina subreddits

- **r/southcarolina** — general state discussion, often political and economic
- **r/Charleston** — Charleston-area urban issues, tech workers, cost of living
- **r/Columbia** — Midlands, USC, state government workers
- **r/greenville** — Upstate, manufacturing, growth, tech-adjacent
- **r/myrtlebeach** — tourism, service economy, retirees

Search queries to try:
- `AI`, `ChatGPT`, `artificial intelligence`, `scam`, `job`, `school`, `deepfake`
- Sort by "Top this month" and "New" for different signal

### National subreddits with AI signal

- **r/artificial**, **r/singularity**, **r/technology** — enthusiast and skeptic perspectives
- **r/ChatGPT**, **r/GPT4** — actual usage patterns and frustrations
- **r/Teachers**, **r/education** — K–12 AI impact
- **r/smallbusiness** — SMB AI adoption and barriers
- **r/Scams** — AI-enabled scam patterns (useful for safety curriculum)

### What to log from online scans

For each relevant post or thread, capture:
1. The topic / framing
2. The dominant emotion (fear, excitement, confusion, frustration, optimism)
3. Specific quotes that reflect how real people talk (not how experts talk)
4. Any questions that keep repeating — those are curriculum hooks
5. Any scam patterns — those go straight into the AI Safety curriculum

### What NOT to do with Reddit data

- Don't quote specific users without permission, especially in grant applications or published materials
- Don't treat r/ sentiment as representative of South Carolina — it skews younger, more urban, more tech-fluent
- Don't skip in-person conversations because you read 50 Reddit threads. Reddit is a supplement, not a substitute.

## After the Interviews: Synthesis Questions

Once you've done 5–10 conversations, block out an hour and answer these in writing:

1. What surprised me most?
2. What did I hear that I was NOT expecting?
3. What words or phrases do community members use that I was not using before?
4. What's the single most common hope I heard? The single most common fear?
5. Based on what I heard, which SCAiL course should launch first — and what might need to change in the curriculum?
6. Who did people trust, and how do I get closer to those people?
7. What's the one thing I would tell my past self before going into these conversations?
