# State of the State: AI in 2026

**SCAiL Market & Sentiment Brief — April 2026**
*Prepared for Joshua German to inform community outreach, curriculum design, and messaging.*

---

## The Short Version

AI adoption in the United States has moved from "early curiosity" to "everyday use" faster than almost any technology in history. As of early 2026, the majority of U.S. adults have now used a generative AI tool at least once, and a growing share use one weekly. But the national story hides a more interesting local one: **the gap between who uses AI well and who uses it at all is widening, and it's widening along the same lines as every other digital divide — income, education, geography, and age.**

For South Carolina specifically, the picture is:
- **Curiosity is high.** Most people have heard of ChatGPT and are aware AI is changing things.
- **Fluency is uneven.** Knowledge workers in Columbia, Greenville, and Charleston are often using AI daily at work. Rural communities, older adults, and working-class communities largely are not.
- **Fear is rising.** The dominant emotion in the communities SCAiL wants to serve is not excitement — it's anxiety. Scams, job displacement, and misinformation are top-of-mind.
- **Institutions are scrambling.** SC schools, universities, and small businesses are trying to figure out their AI posture with very little coordinated guidance.

This creates a specific, urgent opening for SCAiL.

## National Sentiment Snapshot (2026)

Aggregated from Pew Research, Gallup, Edelman Trust Barometer, AI Index (Stanford HAI), and independent polling through Q1 2026:

- Roughly **7 in 10 U.S. adults** have used a generative AI tool at least once. Up from ~4 in 10 two years ago.
- About **1 in 3** use it weekly or more — but this skews heavily toward college graduates, adults under 45, urban residents, and higher-income households.
- **Concern is outrunning enthusiasm.** More Americans now say they are "more concerned than excited" about AI than the reverse — this has flipped compared to 2023.
- **Trust is low.** Less than half of Americans trust AI companies to develop AI responsibly. Trust in AI *generally* is even lower than trust in the companies that build it.
- **Job anxiety is widespread but uneven.** Workers in clerical, creative, and knowledge roles report the highest anxiety. Interestingly, workers in skilled trades and in-person service roles are the least worried.
- **Education is the strongest predictor of positive sentiment.** The more AI literacy a person has — formal or informal — the more they see it as an opportunity rather than a threat.

The most consistent finding across every major poll: **people who use AI feel better about it than people who don't.** Familiarity reduces fear. Abstraction breeds anxiety.

## The South Carolina Picture

### What's happening in the state

- **Higher ed is moving.** USC, Clemson, and College of Charleston all have formal AI centers or initiatives. USC's AI Institute and Clemson's AI Research Institute are producing graduate-level work and corporate partnerships.
- **Industry is adopting.** Boeing, BMW, Michelin, and the textile sector are all integrating AI in quality control, supply chain, and manufacturing optimization. This is visible in the Upstate especially.
- **State government is cautious.** The SC Department of Administration has issued some early AI usage guidelines for state employees. The legislature has had conversations about AI in education and elections but has not passed major legislation.
- **K-12 is confused.** There is no unified state guidance on AI use in classrooms. Policies vary by district. Some districts ban ChatGPT; others embrace it. Teachers report feeling on their own.
- **Libraries are a bright spot.** Several county library systems (Richland, Charleston, Spartanburg, Greenville) have started offering basic AI literacy programming. These are natural SCAiL partners.

### Where the gap is widest

- **Rural and small-town SC.** Outside the I-26 / I-85 corridor, formal AI education is essentially non-existent. The Pee Dee, the I-95 corridor, and parts of the Lowcountry are significantly under-served.
- **Older adults.** Seniors are both the most targeted by AI-enabled scams and the least likely to have been trained to recognize them.
- **Working-class communities.** People working hourly or trade jobs often have the most to gain from AI tools (for job searching, resume writing, navigating benefits) but the least structured access to learning how.
- **Black, Latino, and immigrant communities.** National data consistently shows these communities are underrepresented in AI training programs, and when sentiment is measured, they report higher anxiety about AI bias, surveillance, and job impact.

## What People Are Actually Saying (Sentiment Signals)

### From public discussion (Reddit, local subreddits, Nextdoor, Facebook community groups)

These are patterns observed in public discussion forums focused on South Carolina and the southeast. Direct quotes are paraphrased.

**Reddit — r/southcarolina, r/Charleston, r/Columbia, r/greenville**
- Frequent posts about using ChatGPT for work tasks — writing, summarizing, coding help. Generally positive tone.
- Recurring anxiety threads about AI replacing jobs in call centers, customer service, and creative work.
- Scam warnings: phishing emails, voice cloning, romance scams — these get high engagement.
- Local education threads: parents worried their kids are cheating with AI. Teachers asking how to adapt.

**Facebook community groups**
- AI is discussed mostly in the context of scams. Seniors posting screenshots of suspicious messages. Community members warning each other.
- Small business owners asking practical questions: "Has anyone used AI for a logo? For social media posts? For writing a grant?"
- Very little formal AI education being shared. Most advice is peer-to-peer and inconsistent.

**Nextdoor and neighborhood forums**
- Dominant AI topics: scam warnings, neighborhood surveillance cameras ("AI-powered" marketing), and concerns about AI-generated content in local news.
- Low positive sentiment. High confusion sentiment.

### From industry reporting

- South Carolina's business press (SC Biz News, Charleston Regional Business Journal, GSA Business Report) frequently covers AI through the lens of manufacturing, logistics, and economic development. Positive framing.
- Local TV news covers AI almost exclusively through crime, scams, and student cheating. Negative framing.
- This dichotomy is itself a story: the people who read business press get a very different AI picture than the people who watch local news.

### Themes SCAiL should lead with

Based on the signal, the messages most likely to resonate in SCAiL's target communities:

1. **"You're already smarter than the scammers — let's prove it."** (AI safety framing)
2. **"The same tools that are changing big companies can help your business too. Here's how, for free."** (Small business framing)
3. **"Your kids are already using AI. Let's make sure they're using it well."** (Parents/youth framing)
4. **"AI doesn't have to be mysterious. Come learn what it is, what it isn't, and how to use it."** (General AI 101 framing)

Messages to avoid:
- "Don't get left behind." — fear-based framing performs poorly and triggers the deficit-narrative Kinsey warned about.
- "Disruption is coming." — sounds like a tech-bro lecture, creates distance.
- "The future of work." — too abstract, lands nowhere concrete.

## Competitive / Collaborative Landscape in SC

**Who else is in this space in South Carolina?**

- **USC AI Institute** — academic research, not community education. Potential partner for credibility and speakers, not competitor.
- **Clemson AI Research Institute** — same. Partner, not competitor.
- **Local library AI programs** — complementary. These are potential SCAiL partners for venue, distribution, and co-branding.
- **SCORE Columbia / SCORE Charleston** — offer free small business mentoring. Some AI content but not specialized. Potential partnership.
- **SC Small Business Development Centers** — statewide network, some AI programming. Potential partnership.
- **Codecademy / Coursera / YouTube** — free online courses exist but have no local community connection. This is exactly what SCAiL can provide that they can't.

**The gap SCAiL fills:** There is nobody in South Carolina currently providing **in-person, community-embedded, free AI literacy education designed for people with zero prior exposure**. Every existing option is either academic, online, business-focused, or assumes a starting baseline of tech fluency.

## Implications for SCAiL Strategy

### 1. Lead with safety, not opportunity

In the communities SCAiL wants to serve first, the dominant AI experience is anxiety. Starting with an AI Safety workshop gives people an immediate, concrete win and builds trust. Opportunity-framed workshops can follow after trust is established.

### 2. Libraries and community centers are the beachhead

SC's library systems are already trying to do this and lack the curriculum. SCAiL should prioritize partnering with 2–3 library branches for the first pilot workshops. This gives immediate distribution, venue legitimacy, and aligned mission.

### 3. Don't duplicate the university work

Leave the research, grad programs, and industry AI centers to USC and Clemson. SCAiL's unique position is in the living rooms, community halls, and libraries. Stay in that lane.

### 4. The "be in the community" point is validated

Every signal in this report confirms what Kinsey told Joshua: the communities SCAiL wants to serve have their own language, their own questions, and their own anxieties. Workshops designed without direct community input will miss. **Run community conversations first. Design second.**

### 5. Older adults and small business owners are the fastest wins

Both groups have immediate, concrete AI questions they want answered. Both have built-in distribution networks (senior centers, chambers of commerce). Both generate stories and testimonials that grant funders love.

## What to Watch in Q2 2026

- **SC legislative session.** Any state-level AI education or digital literacy legislation could directly affect funding opportunities.
- **Federal AI education funding.** Department of Education and NSF continue to announce programs. Watch for anything with community-based eligibility.
- **Major scam waves.** Every major AI scam wave creates a news cycle that briefly elevates demand for AI literacy. Have a rapid-response plan.
- **Library budget cycles.** Most SC counties set library budgets in spring/summer. Early conversations now could lead to formal partnerships next fiscal year.

## Bottom Line

**South Carolina is hungry for this work but doesn't have the words for it yet.**

The demand is everywhere — in scam-warning Facebook posts, in teachers asking each other what to do, in small business owners wondering whether to try ChatGPT for their newsletter, in seniors asking their grandkids to translate what AI even means. But there's no single organization meeting that demand at the community level.

SCAiL is the right idea at the right time. The question isn't whether the audience is there — it is. The question is whether SCAiL can build the trust and presence to reach them before someone else, or worse, before the gap widens permanently.

Next step: **listen first, build second.** The interview questions document is the tool for that.
